# WorkflowBuilder stakeholder roles

These seven profiles describe who WorkflowBuilder serves, what matters to them, and how they judge a change. A person can hold several roles. Someone who owns a business process, for example, might also author its Blueprint.

| ID | Role | What matters to them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UP-01 | [Business process owner](business-process-owner.md) | Know whether a process delivered the intended business outcome and adapt it as needs change. |
| UP-02 | [Blueprint author and maintainer](blueprint-author-and-maintainer.md) | Turn a business requirement into a Blueprint they can explain, test and change with confidence. |
| UP-03 | [Process operations team](process-operations-team.md) | See who is affected by a failure, recover the right work and account for what happened. |
| UP-04 | [Platform and cloud team](platform-and-cloud-team.md) | Keep work moving through changes in demand, routine releases and disruption. |
| UP-05 | [Core engine developer](core-engine-developer.md) | Keep orchestration generic, fast and reliable, with behaviour they can reproduce and explain. |
| UP-06 | [Plugin developer](plugin-developer.md) | Deliver focused, reusable tasks that Blueprint authors can understand and use. |
| UP-07 | [Platform product owner](platform-product-owner.md) | Decide where investment in WorkflowBuilder will benefit multiple teams and where there may be a wider market. |

## Shared context

A **Blueprint** describes the work to do. A **Process** is one instance of a Blueprint being carried out. A **Step** is a Blueprint node executed in that Process. A **Plugin** supplies a reusable task that an author can include in a Blueprint.

Most Processes are expected to start when an event in another system triggers an API request. Business process owners and authors bring their knowledge of what happens before and after that request. They may need several Blueprint versions in use at once while other systems adopt a change. Knowledge of which callers rely on each version stays with the people and systems involved.

## Basis and use

The profiles are working drafts drawn from project discovery on 4–5 November 2026. **Working knowledge** covers the business or technical subjects each role understands and the judgements that experience supports. **What guides their decisions** describes how they weigh competing concerns. Individual backgrounds vary. Each profile stands on its own, with links to the professional guidance that informed it.

Needs describe intended outcomes, including some that still require product work. Feedback from people doing these jobs can confirm or refine them. The original discovery distinguishes role needs from more detailed product and engineering decisions. [Discovery notes](../product/discovery-notes.md)

The format follows the practice of describing users' circumstances and the outcomes they seek. [GOV.UK guidance on understanding user needs](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/start-by-learning-user-needs)
